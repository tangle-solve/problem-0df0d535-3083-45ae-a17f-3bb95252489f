### ALGO-000001: P versus NP
- **Subfield:** core complexity
- **Open:** Is P = NP? Equivalently, does every problem with efficiently verifiable solutions also have efficiently computable solutions?
- **Status:** Open since 1971 (Cook–Levin). Notorious as one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems (2000). All known techniques provably fail: relativization (Baker–Gill–Solovay 1975), natural proofs (Razborov–Rudich 1997), algebrization (Aaronson–Wigderson 2009).
- **Type:** theory
- **Why:** The single most consequential question in computer science; separates feasible verification from feasible computation across all of mathematics and cryptography's foundations.
- **Key works:** S. Cook, "The complexity of theorem-proving procedures", STOC 1971; L. Levin, "Universal sequential search problems", Problems of Information Transmission 1973; R. Razborov, S. Rudich, "Natural proofs", JCSS 1997 (DOI:10.1006/jcss.1997.1494); S. Aaronson, A. Wigderson, "Algebrization: a new barrier in complexity theory", TOCT 2009 (arXiv:0806.1347); Clay Mathematics Institute, "P vs NP Problem", claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np
- **Prize:** USD 1,000,000 (Clay Millennium)
- **Related:** ETH/SETH fine-grained bounds, circuit lower bounds, one-way functions existence
- **Aliases:** P=NP; Cook–Levin problem
